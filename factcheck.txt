3. ISSUES FOUND (NEED CORRECTION OR SOFTENING)
======================================================================

3.1 Bad cross-reference: BDNF and exercise (Section 2B, BDNF row)
    Text: "Exercise raises BDNF in animals and some human blood
    measures (Section 11)."
    Problem: Section 11 does not mention BDNF. The word BDNF appears
    only in the new Sections 2A/2B, so the cross-reference implies
    support the document does not contain. The underlying claim is
    commonly reported but was not verified here.
    Suggested fix: remove "(Section 11)" or rephrase to "Exercise is
    reported to raise BDNF in animal studies and some human blood
    measures, though the clinical significance is unclear."

3.2 Stathmin-2 role (Section 2B, TDP-43 row)
    Text: "...reduce proteins needed for axon maintenance, such as
    stathmin-2."
    Source says: stathmin-2 is required for axonal outgrowth and
    regeneration; loss of nuclear TDP-43 causes cryptic splicing of
    STMN2 and impaired axon regeneration (Baughn et al., Science 2023).
    Suggested fix: change "axon maintenance" to "axonal outgrowth and
    regeneration." (STMN2 knockout mice also show neuromuscular
    junction denervation, so "maintenance" is not wrong, but
    "regeneration" is the better-supported wording.)

3.3 Endogenous opioids and naloxone (Section 2A, opioids row)
    Text: "Blocking opioid receptors can reduce the body's own pain
    control and reverse some placebo analgesia."
    Evidence: Reversal of placebo analgesia by naloxone is
    well supported (Benedetti et al., Pain 2023, for open-label
    placebos; earlier studies for deceptive placebos). However, whether
    naloxone alone increases pain is mixed: Gracely et al. (Nature
    1983) reported naloxone hyperalgesia independent of placebo, while
    Benedetti et al. (2023) found naloxone by itself was not
    hyperalgesic in their design.
    Suggested fix: keep the placebo-analgesia statement; change the
    first half to "Blocking opioid receptors can reverse some placebo
    analgesia, and some studies report increased pain sensitivity,
    though findings are mixed."

3.4 Serotonin and pain sensitivity (Section 2A, serotonin row)
    Text lists "greater pain sensitivity" among effects of reduced
    serotonin signaling.
    Problem: Serotonin has both pain-inhibiting and pain-facilitating
    effects depending on receptor and pathway, so this is a
    simplification that is not clearly established for "too little."
    Suggested fix: remove "greater pain sensitivity" or qualify it
    ("pain effects vary by pathway and receptor").

3.5 Nav1.1 localization and the Dravet mechanism (Section 2B)
    Text: "Nav1.1 is concentrated in inhibitory interneurons" and
    "inhibitory interneurons fire less, so networks become
    over-excitable."
    Evidence: VERIFIED as the leading model (Nav1.1 is predominantly in
    parvalbumin interneurons; Dravet interneurons show reduced
    sodium current and firing). Nuances: (a) Nav1.1 is also found in
    some excitatory neuron populations (Ogiwara et al., Hum Mol Genet
    2013); (b) not all inhibitory cells are affected equally, and some
    become MORE excitable (e.g., reticular thalamic neurons).
    Suggested fix: change to "predominantly in inhibitory (especially
    parvalbumin) interneurons" and keep the "leading explanation"
    wording.

3.6 Epinephrine row (Section 2A)
    Text says brainstem epinephrine neurons "contribute to blood
    pressure and arousal-related control."
    Problem: A cardiovascular role for these neurons is well
    established; an arousal role is less clearly supported.
    Suggested fix: say "contribute to cardiovascular control and
    possibly other autonomic functions."

3.7 Oxytocin and milk ejection (Section 2A)
    Text: "Too little oxytocin can impair milk ejection."
    Problem: This is well supported physiologically and in animal
    knockout work, but true primary oxytocin deficiency in humans is
    rare, so "can impair" should not be read as a common clinical
    cause.
    Suggested fix: add "(shown mainly in animal studies; primary
    deficiency is rare in humans)".

3.8 RELN heterozygous variants (Section 2B, Reelin row)
    Text: "One non-working copy can cause a familial form of lateral
    temporal lobe epilepsy."
    Evidence: VERIFIED (Dazzo et al., Am J Hum Genet 2015). Nuances:
    the study found penetrance of roughly 70% in families, the
    variants were missense and reduced Reelin secretion (not simple
    gene deletions), and the condition is now also called autosomal
    dominant epilepsy with auditory features (ADEAF; MedlinePlus).
    Suggested fix: use "certain heterozygous variants ... with
    incomplete penetrance" and mention the ADEAF name.

3.9 Source citations
    a) The StatPearls links NBK539894 (Physiology, Neurotransmitters),
       NBK537267 (glutamate) and NBK526047 (Physiology, Synapse) were
       checked and their content matches the use. NBK470306 returned
       content on the endogenous opioid system, but the exact page
       title "Physiology, Endogenous Opioids" was inferred from content
       and not confirmed. Verify the title before publishing.
    b) The glutamate link is written without "www." (ncbi.nlm.nih.gov);
       it should resolve, but consider using the www form for
       consistency.
    c) The tau review link (PMC5390006) is described generically
       ("a review of tau's physiological and pathological roles")
       because the author names could not be confirmed from the
       search result.

3.10 Cross-reference to Section 7 for Alzheimer's medicines
    Text in 2A (acetylcholine row) points to Section 7 for symptomatic
    Alzheimer's medicines that increase acetylcholine signaling.
    Finding: Section 7 does not name acetylcholine or cholinesterase
    inhibitors. This matches the original document's own wording in
    Section 2 (which makes the same pointer), so the new text is
    consistent with the document, but the specific mechanism is not
    stated in Section 7. The claim itself (some symptomatic Alzheimer's
    drugs act on acetylcholine signaling) is standard and correct.

3.11 Editorial wording
    Section 2A says the tables describe only the "too little" side "as
    requested for this guide." That phrase reads as a note to the
    requester, not as document text. Suggested fix: "These tables focus
    on the 'too little' side and are not a complete picture of each
    system."

4. CLAIMS VERIFIED AGAINST SOURCES DURING THIS CHECK
======================================================================

Neurotransmitters
- Glutamate is the principal excitatory transmitter and the most
  abundant in the brain; excess causes excitotoxicity.
  Source: StatPearls, Physiology, Glutamate (NBK537267).
- Neurotransmitter release is calcium-triggered vesicle exocytosis;
  neurotransmitter alterations are reported in Parkinson's disease,
  schizophrenia, depression, and Alzheimer's disease; transmitters are
  either small molecules (dopamine, serotonin) or neuropeptides
  (enkephalin). Source: StatPearls NBK539894 and NBK526047.
- The endogenous opioid system includes beta-endorphins, enkephalins,
  dynorphins and nociceptin, and modulates pain, emotion and reward.
  Source: StatPearls NBK470306.
- Naloxone reverses placebo analgesia (open-label and deceptive).
  Sources: Benedetti et al., Pain 2023; Gracely et al., Nature 1983
  (see issue 3.3 for the mixed hyperalgesia findings).

Proteins and genes
- SCN1A / Nav1.1: Dravet syndrome is caused mainly by heterozygous
  loss-of-function (haploinsufficiency) mutations; Nav1.1 is
  predominantly in parvalbumin interneurons; interneuron-specific
  deletion causes seizures in mice. Sources: Ogiwara et al. 2007 and
  2013; Cheah et al. 2012 (PMC6672241, PMC3820136); Isom commentary
  (PMC5716123).
- RELN: homozygous mutations cause lissencephaly with cerebellar
  hypoplasia (complete lack of reelin); heterozygous mutations cause
  lateral temporal epilepsy. Sources: Dazzo et al., Am J Hum Genet
  2015; MedlinePlus RELN.
- TDP-43: nuclear loss is a hallmark of ALS/FTD and causes cryptic
  splicing of STMN2, reducing stathmin-2, which is required for axonal
  regeneration. Source: Baughn et al., Science 2023 (PMC10148063).
- Huntingtin: complete knockout is embryonically lethal in mice
  (before day 8.5); normal huntingtin is implicated in vesicle
  transport and BDNF production/transport; loss of normal function may
  contribute to Huntington's disease; therapies that lower huntingtin
  may also lower the wild-type protein. Sources: BMC Neurosci 2008;
  Nat Rev Neurosci 2005 (Cattaneo et al.); PMC5389021.
- Tau: stabilizes axonal microtubules and regulates axonal transport;
  tau deletion in mice gives a surprisingly mild phenotype; disease is
  thought to involve both loss of normal function and toxic gain.
  Sources: Kneynsberg et al., Front Neurosci 2017; PMC4076391;
  Neurodegener 2009 review.

Original-document spot checks
- Siletti et al. (Science 2023): more than three million nuclei,
  approximately 100 dissections, three donors, 31 superclusters, 461
  clusters, 3,313 subclusters, high diversity in midbrain and hindbrain
  neurons. VERIFIED. The document says "about 3.4 million nuclei";
  the published abstract says "more than three million," so the exact
  3.4 million figure was not independently confirmed.
- Cogitate Consortium (Nature 2025): 256 participants; fMRI, MEG and
  iEEG; preregistered adversarial design; both IIT and GNWT predictions
  challenged (lack of sustained posterior synchronization for IIT; lack
  of ignition at stimulus offset and limited prefrontal representation
  for GNWT). VERIFIED. Matches Section 4 and answer 17.

7. LIMITATIONS
======================================================================

- This check used web search snippets and abstracts; full papers were
  not read. Where a snippet did not show a specific number or
  statement, the item is marked as not verified.
- Rare-disease and gene-variant details change as new research
  appears. Clinical or genetic decisions should rely on current
  clinical sources (for example GeneReviews, OMIM, MedlinePlus, and
  treating clinicians), not on this summary.
- A fact check cannot make a simplified summary complete: several
  entries (especially for "too little" of a neurotransmitter in common
  conditions such as depression or anxiety) intentionally describe
  hypotheses, and the document labels them as such.
